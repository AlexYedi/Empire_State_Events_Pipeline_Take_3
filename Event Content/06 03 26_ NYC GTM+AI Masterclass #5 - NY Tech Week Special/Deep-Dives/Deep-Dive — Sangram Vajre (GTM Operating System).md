## 1. The Deep-Dive Post (LinkedIn)

Most strategy offsites end with a deck nobody owns.

The fix I keep coming back to is a one-slide artifact from Sangram Vajre — co-founder of Terminus, the company that coined the ABM category, and now co-founder of GTM Partners. He calls it the GTM Operating System: your entire go-to-market on a single page, built around eight questions a leadership team is supposed to fight over together.

Not eight metrics. Eight questions. Here they are, in plain English:

1. Total Relevant Market — Where can you grow the most?
2. Market Investment Map — Which product(s) create the highest customer value?
3. Brand & Demand — How will you engage your customer with a differentiated PoV?
4. Pipeline Velocity — Which GTM motions get you to your revenue goal faster?
5. Customer Time-to-Value — What's your ROI in the customer's mind?
6. Customer Expansion — How else can you upserve your customers?
7. Revenue Operations — Which GTM metrics drive your business health?
8. Leadership & Management — How do you give your team clarity, alignment, and trust?

The part that reframes how you use it: the goal isn't the right answer.

"There is no right answer to any of these questions… There is no certainty except taxes and death. What this gives you is clarity." — Sangram

The artifact's whole job is to force a healthy debate across the exec team and surface where you quietly disagree. GTM Partners' own research found 78% of GTM leaders say that if they knew what they were doing and why, they'd hit their revenue number. Most teams never get there — not because the answers are hard, but because nobody owns the question.

Which is the second idea worth stealing: "The CEO owns go-to-market, and therefore go-to-market is the business." Marketing-vs-sales spend, build-vs-acquire, EMEA-vs-NA — those aren't departmental calls, they're GTM calls, and they're CEO calls. If everyone owns GTM, no one does.

How to run it Monday:
- Print the wheel. Put all eight on one page.
- Give every exec a silent pass — write their answer to each question alone first.
- Compare. The gaps between answers ARE the agenda.
- Pick a direction the room will stand behind, even if it's imperfect.

Because the closing line is the one that sticks: you'll get better results with a team behind a non-perfect answer than a perfect answer nobody's behind.

Credit where it's due — this is Sangram Vajre's framework (GTM Partners), the 8-pillar expansion of the 4-question MOVE framework from his book. I'm just the guy who couldn't stop thinking about it.

What's the one question on that wheel your leadership team would argue about most?
