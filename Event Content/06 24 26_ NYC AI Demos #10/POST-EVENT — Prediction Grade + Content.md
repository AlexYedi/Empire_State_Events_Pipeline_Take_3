## LINKEDIN POST — Variant A (the forecast post-mortem · documentarian · RECOMMENDED)

I tried to predict a demo night before it happened. I went 1 for 5 — and the four misses were a better education than the one hit.

NYC AI Demos #10 was a "Venture Spotlight": Thrive, First Round, Index, Inspired, and Able each nominated one portfolio company to demo. So beforehand I scored each firm's NYC AI portfolio on momentum — recent raises, launches, hiring, how demo-able the product was — and committed picks.

I got one: Spara (Inspired), the GTM-agent company I'd flagged at high confidence.

The misses are where the lesson is:

→ Thrive sent Pace — agents that run insurance "loss runs" in production. It had raised $46M the month before. The freshest raise in the entire room… and it wasn't even on my list. I'd anchored on the famous names and never dug deep enough to find it.

→ Index sent Uncovr — a 23-year-old's company turning operating-room video into surgical reports. Too new and too quiet for my research to surface.

→ Able sent Spring Health — a mental-health platform that's raised ~$466M. I'd written Able off as "no obvious AI pick" and conceded the slot. Their flagship was hiding in plain sight.

→ First Round's spot went to the host's own company, Pensar. I never modeled that the host might take a seat.

Two things I'm keeping:

1. My model failed on recall, not ranking. Three of four actual picks weren't in my pool at all. A sharp scoring function on an incomplete list is just confidently wrong.

2. VCs don't spotlight their hottest name — they spotlight their highest-conviction one. That ranged from a $466M flagship to a near-unknown founder. I'd optimized for "most demo-able momentum." Wrong target entirely.

The meta-lesson, and it generalizes past demo nights: when you can know the structure of a thing — here, exactly one company per firm — predict the structure first and force a real guess in every slot, especially the one that looks empty. The slot I skipped was the miss I chose.

If you forecast anything noisy — lineups, markets, roadmaps — how do you keep a confident model from running on an incomplete list?

Five VCs each put one company on stage at NYC AI Demos #10. Strip the pitches and they made the same bet: agents doing real work in domains where being wrong is expensive.

The pattern: nobody won on the model. They won on the layer around it — guardrails, domain plumbing, experts in the loop, channel orchestration, the offensive test. And every one of them is building it in New York.

Vertical AI isn't coming. In this room it had already arrived — in healthcare, insurance, revenue, and security.
