**Dana Okafor:** "Let's start easy — walk me through the win you're proudest of."

**Candidate:** "The one I'd pick is my last expansion book at Brightharbor Systems. I carried a $1.8M expansion quota across 14 accounts and finished at 118%."

**Dana Okafor:** "Fourteen accounts. What was the average contract value when you took them over, and where did it land?"

**Candidate:** "About $300K at takeover. By the second renewal the average was $440K, and most of that growth was product attach, not price."

**Dana Okafor:** "The 118% — how much of it was new seats versus attach versus price?"

**Candidate:** "$2.12M closed on the $1.8M quota — roughly half new product attach, a third seat growth, the rest price and mix."

**Dana Okafor:** "When you took the book over, what did the first thirty days look like?"

**Candidate:** "Diagnosis before asks. I pulled three quarters of usage and renewal history before I made a single ask, and I rebuilt the QBR deck with the CS lead so the story the exec saw matched what support was seeing."

**Dana Okafor:** "Tell me about an account you lost."

**Candidate:** "We had one churn where the champion left — the new CIO came in with an incumbent relationship, and honestly there wasn't much anyone could have done."

**Dana Okafor:** "What did you do in the ninety days before they decided?"

**Candidate:** "We kept exec sponsors engaged and the QBRs went fine."

**Dana Okafor:** "And how many accounts did you lose over the three years?"

**Candidate:** "Two of the fourteen."

**Dana Okafor:** *(closing)* "Volume follow-ups are where stories usually wobble. Yours mostly held — we'll pick at the churn one in the book review."
