# Round 2 — Commercial: book review — Northwind Freight Systems (fixture)

> Fixture transcript — fictional company, people, and history (E-09 / YED-256). Interviewer and
> candidate numbers are invented for offline testing.

**Round:** commercial: book review — account-growth evidence, quota-attainment framing · **Persona:** Marcus Feld, Chief Revenue Officer · **Stage:** onsite, round 3 of 3 · **Length:** 30 minutes

---

**Marcus Feld:** "Your book, the last two years. Where did growth come from, and what attests to it?"

**Candidate:** "Every renewal conversation started from the customer's number — their on-time-delivery rate, not our feature list. The book went from $4.2M to $6.2M ARR in two years, and I can walk it line by line."

**Marcus Feld:** "Line by line, then. The expansion quota — attainment, and against what base?"

**Candidate:** "$2.12M closed on a $1.8M quota — 118%. And I want to be careful about the base: the quota was already raised 15% the year before, so this was attainment on a stretched number, not on a floor."

**Marcus Feld:** "So you're really a farmer."

**Candidate:** "If I were, the base wouldn't have moved. Hunting is how I proved the growth motion scales: two new logos landed in my territory last year after the expansion playbook was written — both came from the same referral path the expansion accounts built, and both took the same attach motion inside a quarter."

**Marcus Feld:** "Where's the next two million, and how do you diagnose the book before you commit to a number?"

**Candidate:** "Before I commit a number I diagnose the book three ways: usage depth per seat, renewal-calendar concentration, and where the attach motion already works. Twelve of the fourteen accounts have headroom on those three and four renewals cluster in Q3 — that's where the next two million lives, and the pilot said the attach motion is the lever."

**Marcus Feld:** "Who had to change how they work for the attach motion to land?"

**Candidate:** "The AEs — the motion only moved once the CS handoff put usage data in front of them at week two instead of week six. Adoption of that handoff artifact was the whole ballgame; before it, attach was luck."

**Marcus Feld:** "Where does AI show up in your motion today?"

**Candidate:** "We rolled out an AI agent that fully automates QBR prep — it writes the deck, no human touch."

**Marcus Feld:** "Fully? Your AEs let a generated deck go to a CIO unreviewed?"

**Candidate:** "Mostly — it's about ninety percent there, the AEs just clean it up."

**Marcus Feld:** "What did you test last year, and what would have happened if you had done nothing?"

**Candidate:** "The attach motion. We ran a two-quarter pilot against our do-nothing baseline — six accounts, attach moved from 12% to 19% while the baseline stayed flat. The kill criterion was simple: if the pilot didn't clear a four-point lift in one quarter, we stopped. It cleared eleven."

**Marcus Feld:** *(closing)* "The numbers I can audit. The AI answer I can't — nobody at my table ships 'fully automated' to a CIO."
